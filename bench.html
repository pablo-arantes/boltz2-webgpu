What this can and cannot do: targets up to about 2,000 residues, ligands up to 56 heavy atoms, and two scores that rank rather than measure.

Working out what this GPU can hold…

The target

More than one chain: join them with a colon — CHAINA:CHAINB — and they are folded as separate chains of one complex. Two chains on two lines are not two chains: whitespace is stripped, so they become one chain of their concatenation, and the note beside the box reports how many chains it actually parsed. An alignment search for a multi-chain target is not wired into this page yet; a complex screens as single sequence, which it will say.

The pair representation this model folds is residues² × 128 × 4 bytes, so one tensor alone is 1.91 GiB at 2,000 residues and the trunk needs several at once. Past the ceiling above, the screen is refused before it starts.

Crop to the domain that holds the binding site. That costs nothing in the score: Boltz-2's affinity module reads at most 256 tokens, at most 200 of them protein, within 10 Å of the ligand, so a longer target only changes the structure the score is read off. Keep the whole domain rather than a shell — the crop is folded from scratch with no template, so it has to fold on its own — and keep both domains when the site sits between them.

The molecules

Boltz-2 refuses a ligand over 128 heavy atoms and warns above 56, which was the largest it was trained on. One connected molecule a line: a salt or co-crystal has no single binder to score, so strip counter-ions first.

The numbers

P(binder) and pIC50 come from two heads trained on different data with different supervision. pIC50 is an IC50-like value for ordering molecules, trained on mixed Ki/Kd/IC50 assays — its own authors call it a measure that supports ranking, not a potency to quote. There is no third, combined score and this page will not invent one.

The defaults here are below Boltz-2's own affinity settings of 5 recycles, 200 steps and 5 samples. Screen low to get an order, then re-run the top few properly before believing any single value. A low interface ipTM means the pose is a guess, and so is the affinity read off it.

What this is not

A research tool, for generating hypotheses to test at the bench. It is not a clinical, diagnostic, regulatory or safety instrument, and no output here is evidence that a molecule is safe, effective or worth giving to anything alive. The affinity path is reverse-engineered from upstream rather than vendored, so it can differ from Boltz-2's own numbers; docs/MEASURED.md records every comparison that has been made and what is still open.

Each molecule is folded with its own copy of the target, so every run is superposed on the first run's alpha carbons before it is drawn.

Probability against strength

Point size and opacity are the interface ipTM: a small faint point is a pose the model is unsure of, and an affinity read off an uncertain pose is a guess. The probability axis is the full 0 to 1 rather than the run's own range, so a spread of 0.31 to 0.39 looks like the small difference it is.

Ranked molecules

P(binder) answers whether this is a binder at all. pIC50 is an IC50-like strength for ranking, trained on mixed Ki, Kd and IC50 assays — so it orders molecules, and it is not a potency to quote. Click a column to sort by it.
